## Feeling

Curiosity becomes awe, then hope, then peace. Apocalypse must not leave a child with monsters; its last image and answer belong to the victorious Lamb.

## 1 — Game experience

Three adjacent promises form a mini-arc:

1. **Apocalipsis** — read the symbols, withstand the pressure, reveal the King.
2. **El regreso** — recognize the signs, refuse date-setting, look up with hope.
3. **Milenio** — see what Christ's reign changes in ordinary life: safety, homes, knowledge, one flock.

Each pack keeps the street curve: three untimed recognitions, three 20-second questions, three 15-second bridges, then a 25-point hopeful slam. The loop is anticipation → choose → reveal the scripture meaning → points/streak → next promise.

## 2 — UI design

## 3 — Art direction

- **Apocalypse:** Patmos, seven seals, Lamb light pushing the dragon into mist. Dark, but not horror.
- **Return:** a family sees Christ in ivory-gold clouds. Dark becoming glory.
- **Millennium:** wolf, lamb, child, vines and inhabited homes. Light, lived-in peace rather than sterile utopia.

Thirty generated paintings now give every question its own prophetic scene while preserving the three world signatures. Apocalypse stays indigo and symbolic, Return moves from watchfulness into visible glory, and Millennium opens into inhabited ivory-gold peace.
